What grandma knows about money

The best financial lesson is a story about a bad year.
28 September 2026 · 5 min read

Experience is data

A person who is 75 today has been through currency changes, high inflation, several market crashes and a few property booms. They did not read about it, they lived it. This is information no index chart can give you, because it comes with feelings attached: the fear, the decisions, the regrets.

What courses teach and what elders teach

A course teaches the mechanism: what an ETF is, how fees compound, why time matters. An elder teaches the judgement: when to wait, when to say no, how it felt to sell too soon. The two together are worth more than either one.

How the pair works in practice

In the Humanoya programme the young person does the three-hour training and the elder does none. They meet for a kick-off, then three short weekly coffees where the young person asks three questions and the pair decides one move in a virtual portfolio. The elder never has to touch a computer unless they want to.

What the elder gets back

Time with someone who is curious, a role that matters and, only if they choose, a free first meeting with a Humanoya advisor, with no obligation. Many elders say the best part is simply being asked.
