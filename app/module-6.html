The green option

Lesson 6 of 6 · 10 minutes · Badge: Green investor To do

Lesson filmAbout 45 seconds · captions on

Money can follow your values

A sustainable fund picks companies using environmental, social and governance criteria, often shortened to ESG. Some exclude sectors such as coal or weapons; some favour companies that reduce their emissions; some fund projects such as renewable energy directly. For many young people this is the reason to invest at all, and it is a good one.

Read the label, then read behind it

In France two public labels exist: the Label ISR for socially responsible investment and the Greenfin label for funds that finance the green transition. A label is a start, not a guarantee of impact. Open the fund's sheet, look at its ten largest holdings and ask whether you are comfortable with them. If a "green" fund holds the same names as a plain index, ask why.

Be honest about the trade-off

There is no rule that says green funds earn less, and no rule that says they earn more. Their fees are sometimes higher, which matters after lesson 2, and they may hold fewer companies, which matters after lesson 3. Choose them for the world you want and check the cost and the diversification like any other fund.

In your virtual portfolio

The project lets you hold a world index ETF and a sustainable one side by side. At the end of four weeks you will compare their movements and their fees, and talk about it with your elder. Many elders have strong opinions about what money should and should not pay for.

In this lesson

  • Spot greenwashing

Remember

  • Sustainable funds follow ESG criteria; labels such as ISR and Greenfin exist in France.
  • Read the top holdings: a label is not proof of impact.
  • Check cost and diversification as you would for any fund.
